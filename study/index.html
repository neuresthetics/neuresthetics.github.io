Genius Study

statusdraft · no results yet

Where do the people history remembers as geniuses stand on God, nature and law? This study rebuilds that public record from sources, one person at a time.

Runs on: Grok · one Grok Bot drafts person records from web search and the cited pages; blind audit runs check them (unreviewed drafts)

What it is

  • Lane A describes what the public record shows about each person's view of God and nature. Every quote is sourced and checked. It stands on its own.
  • Lane B is a labeled belief: that learning lawful order early may pay off. It's written so it can fail. Lane A never depends on it, and Lane A never pays for Lane B.
  • Candidates come from five LLM-generated lists (Claude, DeepSeek, Gemini, GPT, Grok), merged into one roster of 1,380 people. 441 of them are core.

Why it's interesting

  • Most talk about “genius and belief” leans on anecdotes or summary labels. Here each person gets a file: primary text or scholarly source, page or paragraph locator, and a score only when the source settles it.
  • The interesting cases are often the awkward ones — a person who names a transcendent God and still describes nature as running on law, or who leaves an axis unsettled. Those stay visible instead of being forced into a tidy story.
  • Five independent model lists disagree a lot: 966 of 1,380 roster people appear on only one list; only 82 appear on all five. That disagreement is part of the record, not smoothed away.

How it works

  • One Markdown file per person (and per belief system), filled from primary and scholarly sources.
  • Five axes (locus, cause, ledger, authority, scope), each 0–4, from an intervening God to lawful, non-intervening order. Every score carries a certainty, capped by the kind of source it rests on.
  • Blind audit runs check drafts against their sources. Method rules and the live progress table live in the repo.

Including weaker scores (sensitivity view)

The main person chart above fades any dot with certainty below 0.7, so you can see what rests on firmer ground. A second view draws the same 89 people at full weight — including the 41 whose best public evidence only clears 0.5 — while hollow rings still mark a single source.

That view is not a claim that weak evidence equals strong evidence. It is an illustration: the cloud of placements does not vanish when you stop visually discounting soft scores. It also marks a hard stop. Analysis is “enough” here when further reading would not move someone onto the grid or change a rule — only deepen a footnote. Complete coverage of every remembered genius is impossible; honest gaps are part of the work. Drafts only — no results yet. Lane A stays descriptive.
