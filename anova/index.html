ANOVA Language

App · AAC word board

A free word board for people who communicate by tapping words instead of speaking. It's a web app for iPad and other tablets, and it works offline once it's installed.

Open Word Board Repo

Get it on your iPad

  1. On the iPad, open neuresthetics.github.io/anova_language_dev_public/ in Safari.
  2. Tap Share (the square with the arrow), then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open Word Board from the Home Screen. It works offline after the first open.

To pick a level or change the look, hold the gear for 2 seconds to open parent settings. Want your own copy to change? Fork the repo and follow the setup guide.

Who it's for

  • Families of nonspeaking or minimally speaking kids who want a board at home, including while they wait on an insurance-funded device.
  • Early readers and spellers who are ahead of their speech: there's a full keyboard with word prediction.
  • Speech therapists, teachers, and tinkerers who want an open, editable board with no account or subscription.

How it works

  • Tap a word to hear it and add it to the message bar. Tap "speak" to say the whole message.
  • Buttons never move. Words are shown in levels (Starter, Middle, Expanded, Sentences, Full, or Custom), and a hidden word leaves an empty cell, so motor memory carries over as the board grows.
  • Colors follow word type (a modified Fitzgerald key).
  • Spelling keyboard (A to Z) with word prediction and a backspace key. Tapping . or ? reads the typed sentence aloud.
  • Paths is a speech-pattern tree you tap through, such as "I want" then milk, or open and close then door, to practice common phrases. It can send the phrase to the message bar.
  • Button look: classic, chunky, tint, or pebble, chosen under Look in parent settings along with contrast, text size, and board color. Only the button surface changes; every word keeps its place and color.
  • All words, paths, and stories are built into the app, so there's nothing to import or download. Photos added on the iPad stay on that device. Nothing is uploaded.

Paths

Pick a pattern, then tap the next word. After "I want", the board offers things to want; after close, it offers door, book, box, and the other things you can close. Each finished path is a short, grammatical sentence that can be said again or added to the message bar.

How the board grows

The board grows with the child without ever moving a button. It has about 420 words in five levels, tiered roughly by age: Starter (about 2), Middle (about 3), Expanded (about 4), Sentences (about 5), and Full (about 6+). The ages are approximate, not a test.

  • Five word levels. On the home board, Starter shows the 49 most-used words, Middle shows 67, and Expanded shows 75 (329 words across the whole board). Sentences adds when and word endings for 78 home words, plus the rest of the "little words" folder. Full adds Conversation, about 420 words in all: a "time" folder, thinking and school words, and social phrases.
  • Word endings: tap -s, -ing, -ed, or 's after a word to change it, so jump becomes jumping, cry becomes cries, go becomes went, and child becomes children. Delete takes the ending off first.
  • Updates never surprise the child. A saved level carries over, and a board that was on Full before 1.4.0 stays at Expanded, so Sentences and Conversation words appear only when a parent picks those levels.
  • Levels can apply inside folders too (it's off by default). At Starter, for example, the animals folder shows 42 of its 89 animals. Sentences and Conversation words always follow the level.
  • Single words can be turned on or off in parent settings, so you can add one new word at a time ahead of the next level.
  • Buttons never move. A hidden word leaves an empty cell, and new words only fill empty cells, so motor memory carries over.
  • Opposites sit together: more and less, up and down, open and close, big and little, hot and cold, yesterday and tomorrow, before and after.
  • New words are added to the app's word list and arrive with the next update. Photos for one child can be added on the iPad and stay on that device. An opt-in usage log, kept on the device, shows which words get used, so you know when to show more.

Why it's free

Dedicated speech devices often cost several thousand dollars, and commercial iPad AAC apps cost a few hundred. This one runs on a tablet you may already have. It's a home option, not a replacement for an evaluation by a speech-language pathologist, and it's not a medical device.

How it's built

Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No build step, frameworks, ads, analytics, or network calls. Installs as a home-screen web app and works offline. MIT license.

Status

Version 1.6.0, started September 2026 and actively developed. Open it from the live link and add it to the Home Screen; updates arrive on their own. Recent changes:

  • Paths builds short, grammatical sentences, checked by an automatic grammar audit across every path.
  • Age-tiered word levels: Starter, Middle, Expanded, Sentences, and Full (about ages 2 to 6+), with an optional Starter big-buttons view.
  • Share and feedback live in parent settings (share button, copy link, QR code, and an email you write), never on the child's board.
  • Button looks: classic, chunky, tint, and pebble, chosen under Look in parent settings.

About the name

ANOVA is a nod to analysis of variance, the statistics term. Language learning varies a lot from person to person, and the board is built to adapt to that. The app itself doesn't do any statistics.
