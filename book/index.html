Working title: Freedom of Necessity: God, Brain, and the Order of the Mind. A book written axiom by axiom, in the geometric order of Spinoza's Ethics. It starts from "I exist" and argues that there is one order, Nature, and that the mind is the body's own organization described from within. Freedom isn't escape from causes. It's knowing where a cause comes from and acting from that knowledge. It's part of the Neuresthetics research.

Spinoza's own manuscript of the Ethics is lost; only copies survive. This is an attempt to run his method again from first principles, with digital intelligence: the author sets the starting point and the rules, and Grok writes and checks each step.

Read the origin story first: Foreword — How this text came to be. The companion preface, On a Book Without Its Original, says what the book takes from Spinoza and what it adds.

The current readable face is the B1 winner dual render — 251 verses on one chain, two doors (technical and poetic), same numbers and the same index labels (entry ids).

Why two tracks

Spinoza's geometric form is exact, and the living sense of the Ethics is not always visible under the proofs — he says as much himself. The dual tracks keep one chain and open two doors: the technical track shows the joints (deps, caps, cites, entry ids); the poetic track carries the same claims in plain modern lines. Same V001–V251, same labels. Elegance and structure, not two books.

How that face was chosen: variation and hardness-to-vary ran on the combined chain (not track-vs-track). One BASE winner was confirmed; the full live set was expanded off it; then that winner received the dual render.

V003 · A1. I exist. Doubt it, and the doubting is already here — a lamp you cannot switch off by staring at it.

V013 · P3. Freedom doesn't need that you could have done otherwise, given all that came before. It needs that what you did came from you.

V009 · P1. I am a whole in my own skin, and a part in the city that holds me. Both true, like a brick that is wall and is clay.

How it's written

Each entry is a definition, axiom, or proposition, and cites the entries it builds on. Every entry is tagged by kind (true by definition, backed by evidence, or a model) so philosophy can't pass itself off as science. The arc starts from the barest claim: an I exists, finds it is a body, finds the body is a whole, and finds it is a member of something larger.

How it's checked

A claim and its negation are each argued at full strength, and only what survives on checked evidence is kept. Before a run, each side has to say what result would prove it wrong. No entry can be more confident than its weakest support. The book is written and checked with Grok. There are no harness scores yet beyond A1.
