Part one

RestoKit: the framework

RestoKit is the framework: the job stages (phases) and the modules that load into an AI model. The tools built on it have their own section further down.

Old way vs. with agents

Same roles. Fewer handoffs. People move from producing the work product to approving it.

Old way Produce, then chase

  1. Tech clicks through dashboards and types notes
  2. PM collects missing data after the stop
  3. Estimator builds the estimate from scratch
  4. Bill gets assembled after the fact

With agents Draft live, then approve

  1. Tech talks on site; agent drafts scope, billables, and equipment checks with photo timestamps
  2. PM approves — file triage, not data hunting
  3. Estimator approves or modifies the draft
  4. Owner reviews the job when they want

The role shift: every seat moves from producing the paperwork to approving it. Fewer steps, fewer handoffs, less fatigue — and the billable trail is written while the work happens.

Why a framework

This isn’t just AI. It’s AI plus a framework of crystallized restoration knowledge: real field judgment from real jobs, structured so every agent makes calls the way an experienced restorer would instead of guessing.

  • Near-perfect documentation, at low effort. With RestoKit, every person on the job has a Grok Bot agent, not a chat window. Each agent runs on its own computer, remembers the job, and keeps working in the background while you keep working. It takes input from several people and other bots at once and keeps every thread straight, so the tech’s voice notes, the lead’s calls, and the PM’s approvals all land in the right place. That gets job documentation to a near-perfect standard at a fraction of today’s effort. The restoration knowledge base is the foundation, and that’s what makes it trustworthy instead of the agent guessing.
  • Training folded into the work. No matter how long you’ve been in restoration, there’s something new to learn every day. Any tech, lead, or PM can ask about whatever is in front of them, right on the job: “Why are we pulling this pad?” or “What’s dry standard for this subfloor?” They get the answer and the reasoning behind it, drawn from the collective knowledge of the trade, so learning speeds up for rookies and veterans alike. Today it’s comprehensive enough to run mitigation. It doesn’t hold the infinite multitudes yet, but that’s exactly what AI can finally handle, and the kit grows toward it with every job.
  • Ready to send. By the end of a job, RestoKit holds the voice logs plus all measurements, materials, and equipment placements needed for a structured PDF you can send to an insurance company. With your company’s approved price lines, that package can stand on its own without a separate estimating platform.
  • Anyone can pick it up. Task lists stay hand-editable. Someone not using RestoKit can open one, see what’s done, half done, and still to do, pick up where the last person left off, and check a box. Crews don’t have time to write long lists, and hand-written lists are sloppy and rarely read.

A living shared standard

Every employee’s Grok Bot agent runs on the same decision standard. As the crew learns, the kit picks up their edge cases and the solutions they agree work best.

That standard feeds real-time decisions — including justifications to an insurer about why a line is billable — with the reason attached. Two shops on the kit can say they make trade decisions the same way, without waiting on the next printed edition of a standards book.

  • Edge cases stay in the kit The rare ones don’t come up often enough to stay top of mind. The standard carries them, so you carry less of that inventory in your head.
  • Field-proven editions Growth still goes through review by experienced field techs. Once an edition is verified, everyone gets it at the same moment.
  • Tradecraft plus context The network also adapts to material costs, the economy, and laws — and stays tied to the numbers so billable items come out while the work is happening.

Scope to billing, made easy

Every billable item carries a real reason, down to the specifics of that job. Adjusters get a clear window into the services you provided and why — not a stack of lines with no trail.

That is the claim trail from the stop, written for the person who has to defend it.

  • The claim trail rides with the work Photos, moisture, why the equipment is there and where it sits, and a short note happen on the stop, not at the end of the day.
  • Carriers read the data, not the logo Insurance carriers care what’s in the file, not which software made it. A file laid out close to the format carriers already see for that job type gives them no reason to push back, because it carries all the required data, and usually better data than most restoration companies send today. Well-organized files move through review faster, which means less back-and-forth and faster payment.

How it works

Set it up once. After that, you talk and you approve.

  1. Connect it once

    We set up your agents and connect them to the software you already run, like your job-file system, so each role gets the views and approvals it needs. Your crew adds the Grok Bot app. You still work in your existing software, but production gets cleaner and less tedious, and anyone who prefers working in it by hand still can, just as they do today. That also opens the job file to people who aren’t technical: if you can talk, you can document.

  2. Walk the job and talk

    Say what you see as you go: “Kitchen, Cat 2 supply line, LVP over ply, wet 4 feet up the base.” The agent keeps the notes and matches them to your photos by time, so you aren’t typing and clicking on site.

  3. Ask when you need a second brain

    “Remove or dry this LVP?” “How many air movers for this room?” “Is the subfloor at dry standard?” You get a short, field-ready answer grounded in IICRC S500 and S520, with the why if you want it.

  4. Approve what it drafts

    Scope lines, moisture logs, and notes come back as drafts with the reason for each one. You approve, edit, or reject, and nothing gets written to the file without you.

  5. Done as you go

    No more writing reports at the end of the day. Approved work goes straight into the build and the job file, so it’s finished while you’re still on site. The PM, the estimator, and the owner see the same current record, and the billing already has its reasons attached by the time the job closes.

What’s inside

  • Five gated phases From Survey & Estimate to Closeout, so safety steps don’t get skipped. Hold is a status a job can enter from any phase.
  • Modules Category, remove vs. dry, containment, negative air, equipment sizing, mold, crawlspaces, hazard sampling, and more.
  • Base science RH, temperature, GPP, vapor drive, wicking, material behavior, dry standards.
  • Field lessons From daily job logs, added only once they prove out on real jobs.

For the shop

Not more software. Agents that pilot what you already use.

RestoKit is not another app to learn, license, or click through. It is a restoration knowledge backbone plus Grok Bot agents that help your people pilot the software you already run — any tool with an API.

The agents are an intelligent bridge between restoration knowledge and the decisions that produce scopes, billable items, estimates, and bills. That puts your trainer and standards keeper in the same line as the people writing the scope.

  • Compress the dashboard fatigue Huge screens of buttons put a speed limit on how good documentation can get. An agent on a restoration-logic framework builds scope sheets in real time, handles email, and talks to your other apps.
  • Answer the “no more software” reflex This is not LiDAR or another feature pack. It is coordinated information flow between apps and people on Grok Teams, backed by the kit.

Less clicking. More field time.

Running a documentation app fluently takes a huge number of taps a year, even with heavy voice-to-text. Working by audio takes that fatigue off the tech and gives them an agent that recalls restoration knowledge on the spot.

The same agent calculates cubic footage for quick equipment checks. Those numbers flow straight into billable items when the equipment is placed, and they stay united with the photo data by timestamp.

By our field estimate: operating Encircle fluently can cost a technician roughly $1,000–$2,000 a year in button-pushing time. That figure is Jason’s field estimate from shop experience — not a published study.

Customer service: every party on the same page

While the tech talks to the agent on site, the homeowner — often in the next room — hears exactly what’s affected, why, and what’s going on with the environment. It’s the same information they’d see in their file, so they don’t have to request and read it after the fact. They can be live with you on the project if they want.

They also hear that every billable item comes from the reason for the work. That is a streamlined, high-quality information experience for the person whose home it is.

Time pressure in this trade can push documentation to the edge. When honest, reason-backed documentation is faster than cutting corners, everyone wins.

Who it’s for

  • Tech The job flow in order, with safety checks called out when they matter: category, electrical, crawlspace gas.
  • Lead Containment, negative air, equipment sizing, and crew and labor calls.
  • PM Hold status, work authorization, daily drying readings, and verification before teardown.
  • Estimator The Phase 0 survey and scope, remove-vs-dry and category calls, plus documentation that backs up the scope.
  • General manager Every job’s status across crews and PMs in one current record: what’s stuck, what’s on hold, and who’s on what. You don’t have to chase updates.
  • Owner Real-time vision over the whole company. Query profit and loss whenever you want, or drill into any job down to the photos just taken.

What it is

RestoKit is a structured JSON kit for anyone running a restoration job. It’s grounded in IICRC S500 (water) and S520 (mold) principles, and written the way a senior tech talks a job through.

It doesn’t replace years on the job. It carries lessons from people who’ve spent decades in the trade. Its main job is memory: the rare edge cases that don’t come up often enough to stay top of mind.

Where it’s headed: every restoration field

Water and mold make up most restoration jobs, and that’s where RestoKit’s field knowledge runs deepest. It already covers contents at a basic level, and fire jobs share most of the same structure. A fire job is largely a water and mold job with different cleaning and demo. The plan is to deepen each of those fields and grow the same standard across the whole trade, built from real jobs and through the same distributed, field-proven review. That way one company can run every kind of loss on one standard with one set of agents.

Main page, all sections collapsed

The board's front page. One Ops panel with a collapsible section per tool; each header shows a live count or status (jobs on board, trucks staffed, stops today).

  • Collapse all / Expand all
  • Sync stamp and job count in the header

Personnel

Who is on the crew today, their role and the truck they're in.

  • Role and default vehicle per person
  • Tap a name for skills, licenses and notes
  • Local-only edits, never synced

Ties in: Feeds Vehicles and Dispatch so crews are suggested from people who are actually available.

Vehicles

Seats people into trucks for the day.

  • Drag or tap to seat a person
  • Crew suggestions from today's jobs, two-person jobs first
  • Return to defaults / set defaults

Ties in: Uses Personnel plus the job board's due stops; the seats drive Dispatch and the Labor ratio.

Job Digest

A job-by-job briefing so office and field start from the same picture. Shown collapsed here; the header chip names the three fields.

  • Get in: what came in on the job
  • State: where the job stands now
  • Next: the next action

Ties in: Same jobs as the job board, folded into one short briefing per job.

Job board

Every open job as a card in the lane for its stage, from call to close.

  • Lanes (Call/Inspect, Work Authorization, Hazard testing, Abatement, Demo, Dry out, On hold, Close) or table view
  • Needs-action filters: missing work auth, asbestos/lead not negative, lockbox and spider placement
  • Locked by default; unlock to edit

Ties in: Lanes follow the kit's phase sequence. Card data comes from the Encircle digs through the tower API.

Labor ratio

Due stops divided by the stops the crew can make today, as a plain call: OK, busy or too busy.

  • Two-person vs solo check coverage
  • Stuck jobs by lane
  • Short explainer of what the ratio measures

Ties in: Built from the job board and today's Vehicles seats.

Triage

An intake script for the office on a new loss call, most urgent first.

  • Tap what the caller says; the board suggests urgency
  • Only asks what a caller can answer; the tech measures the rest
  • Writes are disabled when offline (as in this demo)

Ties in: Questions and urgency rules come from the kit's intake guidance; a filled intake becomes a new job card.

Dispatch

Builds the day's routes from the trucks going out and the stops due.

  • Suggest crews from jobs, compute routes, clear
  • Per-device settings for crews and special conditions
  • Optional resto_kit_bot review of the plan

Ties in: Uses Vehicles, the job board and the Map; route order shows as numbered pins.

Map

Job pins on a map, colored by lane.

  • Lane filters, fit to jobs
  • Show today's routes with truck stop order
  • Rings mark jobs that need a stop today

Ties in: Pins come from the job board; routes come from Dispatch. (Demo data here has no pins placed.)

Hazard survey

Jobs that still need asbestos or lead testing.

  • Pending / unknown / positive status per job
  • Close and On hold skipped
  • Mark a request locally with Update

Ties in: Read-only from board.json; results gate Demo per the kit's hazard rules.

ScopeBot

The billable line-item list ScopeBot scopes against, in order of work.

  • 10 categories, 114 items

Templates

PM analysis

A project manager's view of what has stalled.

File quality
