split_load_sim

graph modelsworking name

Graph-growth models that connect the study and the book, descended from graphtacular (2019). Model outputs, not findings.

Runs on: No model · seeded Python graph models

What it is

split_load_sim uses graph theory as a model of the structure of the brain and mind, to connect two projects: the Neuresthetics Genius Study (v8), which collects sourced records of remembered geniuses (Lane A) and states a labeled belief model (Lane B), and the book Freedom of Necessity, whose axiom A2 says the organization of the body's cells produces the mind.

The graphs are models of how a mind's maps could be structured and how they grow. They are not brain data, they are not neuroscience findings, and a vertex is not a neuron.

First use: Lane B inside a model

Lane B (H1, cultivation) says early exposure to the circle (Deus sive Natura) plus geometric form reduces split-model load. The repo asks two questions:

  • If you build Lane B's assumptions into a growth model, do they produce the effect Lane B claims, inside the model?
  • Could the data Lane A collects tell H1 apart from H2 (selection) and H3 (attractor)?

It does not test whether H1 is true of real people. Results are model outputs, not findings. Everything is synthetic: no real Lane A record is read. The exception-prior strand is built with a reserved map joined by one edge, so some of its "split" measures differ from H1 by construction.

How growth works

One seed vertex starts at step 1 of a shared instruction list, the strand. Each round, every running vertex runs its current instruction: branch (definition → consequence), complete_cluster, close, link_region, fork_reserved (a second map joined by a bottleneck edge) or bridge_regions (late merging). Randomness is seeded, so the same strand and seed always give the same graph. Results are compared against null models.

Where the strand idea comes from

The strand comes from graphtacular, a C# engine from 2019 where every vertex runs a shared, numbered instruction list, like a genome. split_load_sim keeps the idea and fixes its weak points: typed, validated instructions instead of string matching, one kernel rule for every vertex, and exports only to a directory you choose.

Status

  • The candidate measures of split-model load are provisional. The study names split-model load but doesn't define a measure for it.
  • The H2 and H3 models and the discrimination analysis are proposed and synthetic. They depend on mappings and parameter ranges Jason has not signed off.
  • Open decisions are tracked in docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md.
